(My Role)

  • Designed and built the app end to end during the hackathon, from sign-in to diagnosis.
  • Connected the plant-disease diagnosis to the Plant.id API, from camera or gallery to a ranked result.
  • Built the farmer map with a 10 km radius and organic and crop filters.
  • Set up Firebase for accounts, farmer profiles and chat, with offline persistence.

(The Problem)

Small farmers often face crop disease alone. Expert advice is far away, and the farmer down the road who solved the same problem last season is a stranger. By the time help arrives, the harvest has already taken the hit.

(The Idea)

Put three things in every farmer's pocket: a way to identify a sick plant from a photo, a map of who farms nearby and what they grow, and a direct line to ask them.

(The Result)

A working app demoed in front of the jury, awarded 2nd place. The code was later cleaned up and published: API keys moved to environment variables, duplicate Firebase setup merged, and the types tightened.

Approach

01

Diagnose First

The most urgent need gets its own tab: take a photo, get likely diseases with a confidence score in seconds.

02

Local by Default

Location powers everything: the closest farmers come first, distances are shown in kilometres, and the map opens on your area.

03

Built for Tunisia

Profiles use the country's 24 governorates, local crops and irrigation types. The app was planned in Arabic and English.

04

Ship in a Weekend

Expo, Firebase and React Native Paper let a hackathon timeline focus on the farmer's problem, not on infrastructure.

(Part 01)

One App.
Three Tools.

Diagnosis, discovery and conversation in five tabs. Each one answers a question a farmer actually asks: what's wrong with my plant, who can help, and how do I reach them?

(System 01 · Plant Diagnosis)

A photo in, an answer out

The farmer takes a photo or picks one from the gallery. The image is sent to the Plant.id health-assessment API, which returns the likely diseases ranked by probability. A healthy plant gets a clear “Healthy Plant” result.

Input
Camera or gallery, cropped to 4:3
Engine
Plant.id health assessment (crops model)
Output
Disease names with confidence percentages

(System 02 · Farmer Map)

Who farms near me?

The map centres on the farmer and draws a 10 km circle. Organic farms show as green pins and conventional farms as red. Layer switches filter by organic only or by crop type. Tapping a pin opens the farmer's card with their crops and distance.

(System 03 · Network & Chat)

Advice from the next field over

The home feed lists the ten closest farmers with their farm name, distance and crops, next to a strip of plant information from the Perenual database. From there it's one tap to a direct message, a group chat, or the plant-group pool, where you swipe right to join a group.

(System 04 · Accounts & Offline)

Works where the signal doesn't

Farmers sign in with email, Google or as a guest, then build a profile with their governorate, crops, farm size, experience and irrigation. Fields often have weak coverage, so Firestore offline persistence and local storage keep key data available without a connection.

(Identity)

Green Tunisia Network

The project was presented under the GTN emblem: a farmer framed by Tunisian ornament, in the greens of the fields and the reds and ochres of local tilework. The aim was to feel familiar and local, not like a generic tech app.
